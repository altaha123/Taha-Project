How the Altaha Score works

The Altaha Score is a 0 to 100 research ranking of an NSE-listed company, with its arithmetic left open. It is not a forecast, and it is not a probability of profit. This page says how the number is put together, what the weights are, and what it cannot tell you.

How the number is assembled

Raw observations are ranked against business-model peers, averaged within a signal group, then within a pillar, then weighted for the holding period you asked about and shrunk towards the middle where the evidence is thin. A missing input is never counted as a zero: it is withheld, and the confidence falls instead.

  1. Observe. Each factor is measured from reported numbers and prices, oriented so higher is better.
  2. Rank. Every factor becomes a percentile against companies with the same business model, never against the whole market.
  3. Group. Related signals average together first, so adding a second trend metric does not quietly double the weight of trend.
  4. Weight. The eight pillars are combined using the holding period’s weights, below.
  5. Shrink. A score built from fewer inputs is pulled towards the middle, because a confident number from thin evidence is the expensive kind of wrong.

The eight pillars and their weights

These are disclosed priors, not estimated truths. No weight search was run to produce them. A day trader and a ten-year holder are asking different questions of the same company, which is why one set of weights could not serve both. Each column adds to 100.

Reading the number

Because every score is a peer percentile pulled towards 50 where the evidence is thin, scores do not spread evenly from 0 to 100. Most companies sit between 40 and 55. The word beside a score follows where scores actually fall:

Strong
60 and above
Good
55 to 60
Average
40 to 55
Weak
33 to 40
Poor
under 33

A company with little data lands in the Average band on purpose. That is the score saying it does not know, which is different from saying the company is ordinary.

What it is not

  • Not a forecast. A high score describes the evidence available today. It says nothing about what the price will do.
  • Not a probability. 82 is not an 82% chance of anything. It is a rank against peers, on disclosed weights.
  • Only partly measured. The three price pillars were tested on ten years of NSE prices. The five business pillars have no such history to test against, and remain judgement.
  • Not comparable across versions. An old composite and a v4 score are different measurements and must never be pooled as one record.

Which companies get a score

The score ranks NSE-listed companies. Each universe scan ranks a cohort of companies together, on one date. A company outside that cohort is scored when its page is opened, against the same cohort and as of the same date, so it gets the score it would have had inside the scan. A US listing is shown as not ranked, because there is no peer group behind it to rank against.

Every figure the score reads comes from the company’s own filings with the exchange and from traded prices. Where a filing is missing, the factor is left out and the confidence printed beside the score falls.
